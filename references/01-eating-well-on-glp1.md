---
title: Eating well while taking a GLP-1 medication
source: Joint nutrition advisory from ACLM, ASN, OMA and TOS (2025), "Nutritional priorities to support GLP-1 therapy for obesity"
status: DRAFT, pending clinician review
---
GLP-1 and GLP-1/GIP medications (such as semaglutide and tirzepatide) reduce appetite and slow how quickly the stomach empties. Many people eat much less than before, which makes the quality of each meal more important.

Protein: Eating less overall can lead to loss of muscle along with fat. Prioritizing protein at each meal (for example eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, fish, chicken, tofu, beans) helps protect muscle. Many people find it helpful to eat the protein portion of the plate first. Your dietitian will set an individual protein target with you.

Small, slow meals: Because the stomach empties more slowly, smaller portions eaten slowly are usually more comfortable. Stopping at the first signs of fullness helps prevent nausea and discomfort.

Nutrient-dense foods: With a smaller appetite, choose foods with more nutrition per bite: vegetables, fruits, whole grains, legumes, lean proteins, and dairy or fortified alternatives. Limit foods that are high in added sugar, fried, or very greasy.

Hydration: Reduced appetite often means reduced thirst and less fluid from food. Sipping fluids throughout the day helps prevent dehydration and constipation.

Muscle-strengthening activity, along with adequate protein, helps preserve muscle and physical function.
