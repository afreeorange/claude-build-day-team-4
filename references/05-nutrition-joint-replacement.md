---
title: Nutrition before and after hip or knee replacement
source: American Academy of Orthopaedic Surgeons (AAOS) OrthoInfo patient guidance; lifestyle medicine program materials
status: DRAFT, pending clinician review
---
Before surgery: Excess body weight puts extra load on the hip and knee joints and is linked to a higher risk of complications such as wound problems and infection. For this reason many surgeons ask patients to reach a weight or BMI goal before joint replacement. Your surgical team sets your specific goal.

Good nutrition before surgery supports healing: enough protein, plenty of fruits and vegetables, and good blood sugar control if you have diabetes.

After surgery: Your body needs protein and overall nutrition to heal the incision and rebuild strength. Regular meals with a protein source help, even if your appetite is low. Fluids and fiber help prevent constipation, which is common after surgery and with pain medicine.

Physical therapy exercises are a key part of recovery. Eating enough protein supports the muscle strength you are rebuilding.

Contact your surgical team if you notice increasing redness, warmth, swelling or drainage at the incision, fever, or new calf pain or swelling.
